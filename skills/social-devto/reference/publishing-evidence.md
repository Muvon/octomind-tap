## Overview

Open this reference when preparing editor metadata, AMA or challenge material, or a measurement plan. Treat dated observations as evidence for a bounded hypothesis. Use the main skill's policy gate before preparing any promotional publication.

An AMA or challenge entry's replies are still comments: current DEV guidelines prohibit AI-generated comments except basic translation, grammar/syntax improvement, and assistive tools. Keep response planning to factual notes for the human; disclosure tiers for articles do not create a comment-generation exception (official, DEV AI Guidelines, checked 2026-09-05).

## Editor and discovery checks

The verified editor evidence establishes Markdown, Liquid embeds, and a maximum of four tags (official, DEV Editor, 2026-09). It doesn't establish the title-character ceiling, cover dimensions, or every front-matter field's current type. Don't insert remembered limits into a publishing package.

Use this field inspection list before preparing front matter. It is a review procedure, not a verified current schema (directional).

| Field to inspect | Package decision and preview check |
|---|---|
| `title` | Clear technical promise; inspect current validation and rendered card for truncation |
| `published` | Keep the draft unpublished until approved for publication; confirm the editor's current boolean handling |
| `tags` | Verify accepted spelling and input syntax, relevant audience, and the documented cap |
| `cover_image` | Use an accessible image URL if supported; check the editor's current dimensions/aspect advice and crop |
| `canonical_url` | Identify the actual original article; don't point to a generic homepage |
| `series` | Confirm exact series title and membership before adding an installment |
| `description` | Supply a plain summary; inspect current field handling and preview rather than copying the opener blindly |

DEV supports canonical URLs, RSS import, and series; after the second series entry its title and Table of Contents appear on entries (official, DEV Writing, 2026-09). Inspect an imported draft for assets, links, and code formatting before publishing (directional).

DEV's guide documents GitHub repository/issue/gist, CodePen, and Twitter embeds (official, DEV Editor, 2026-09). Documentation doesn't guarantee that a particular external embed renders now. Test the actual artifact and retain its source link; don't assert unverified video or newer social-embed support (directional).

The weekly newsletter's Top 7, writing challenges, and writing-streak badges at 4, 8, and 16 consecutive weeks remain documented (official, DEV Engagement, 2026-09). These are recognition surfaces, not evidence that challenge posts dominate discovery. Follow a sustainable publishing plan instead of stretching work for a badge (directional).

Designated curators can award at most five Gems daily. Author badges mark 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, and 64 Gems; curation increases author trust and affects discovery through a dedicated Curated feed, with long-term weights evolving (official, DEV Gems, 2026-09). Record awards if received; don't promise them or infer a comment-ranking boost from unverified discussion replies (directional).

Organizations support branding, article CTAs, views/reactions/comments, follower notifications, and unlimited contributors (official, DEV Organizations, 2026-09). The verified page doesn't establish price or CTA-click analytics. Complete the human author's profile and retain proper credit; the Organization guide recommends a sidebar CTA and canonical attribution for reposts (official, DEV Organization Guide, 2026-09).

Don't assume a sponsored-placement label, Billboard format, poll, video post, or short update exists in the current account workflow. Inspect the applicable publishing surface and terms before drafting for it. Until verified, use a documented article or discussion structure and disclose sponsorship plainly; don't present an editorial disclosure recommendation as a verified paid-product specification (directional).

## Evidence for editorial experiments

### Account studies

| Verified observation | Action and boundary |
|---|---|
| Morning slots outperformed same-day evening slots; Tuesday and Thursday led the reported mornings (measured, Jackson, n=30 posts from a single account, 2026-02). | Test morning against another feasible slot in the intended audience's timezone. The report doesn't establish a transferable timezone or a randomized timing effect (directional). |
| Series entries averaged 216 views and 9.7 reactions against 94 views and 4.2 reactions for standalone posts (measured, Jackson, n=30-post account sample, 2026-02). | Use series when the subject recurs, then compare with your own baseline. Selection and topic differences can explain the association (directional). |
| The highest reported length-band average was 189 views at 1,800–2,200 words (measured, Jackson, n=30-post account sample, 2026-02). | Test that band only when the explanation needs the space; it supplies no universal minimum or maximum (directional). |
| Reported average views increased from 89 in the opening week to 203 in the closing week as schedule consistency rose (measured, Jackson, n=30-post account sample, 2026-02). | Keep a sustainable calendar and track longitudinally. Don't treat the author's separately stated percentage uplift as derivable from these averages or attribute change to cadence alone (directional). |
| Practical local-AI how-tos repeatedly drew about five times the views of deep security posts (measured, Espitia, n=67 posts from a single author, 2026-07). | Lead through a common workflow problem, then explain the niche expertise it exposes. This doesn't justify unrelated trending tags (directional). |
| Comments appeared after the author replaced generic closing prompts with specific answerable questions (measured, Espitia, n=67-post self-report, 2026-07). | Ask for a bounded experience only when the answer helps; omit the question when the article already finishes its job (directional). |
| A continuing narrative produced 445 reactions and 251 comments across 15 posts over 20 days, with 7–86 reactions per entry (measured, Xu, n=15-post series, 2026-06). | Test recurring technical stakes and consistent packaging. Retain unresolved costs; topic, audience, and narrative changed together (directional). |
| The same author planned to assemble the stories into a book and requested reader blurbs (measured, Xu, n=single author recap, 2026-06). | Consider a collected reference or downstream asset after readers show interest. A plan isn't a published asset or proof of sales (directional). |
| An author's first 16 posts averaged 1.6 reactions; the next 12 followed a switch to active participation, with increased reactions/followers reported but no later average (measured, ndcodes, n=28-post self-report, 2026-08). | Read and reply as part of publishing. Don't promise an engagement lift or rename the author's reported Trustee Member status (directional). |
| A founder reported 7 reactions on a direct launch post, later weekly writing and challenge participation, and more than 20,000 followers after a year (measured, Javz, n=single founder recap, 2026-07). | Build a useful body of work before relying on an announcement. The account growth isn't a forecast, conversion result, or causal test of challenges (directional). |
| A team described a failed cold launch followed by DEV articles, architecture documentation, and an external contribution (measured, Zwiserfit, n=single team recap, 2026-07). | Prepare technical proof around the announcement and assess whether people can use it. Don't import another platform's launch sequence or voting rules into DEV (directional). |

These self-reports are selected accounts with limited controls. Treat publication dates as source dates, not proof that the described activity happened that month. None supplies a platform-wide median.

### Public surfaces and model scope

A Top Month snapshot included a 13-minute Git article at 254 reactions and 110 comments, and a 2-minute discussion at 121 reactions and 141 comments (measured, DEV Top Month, n=2 observed posts, 2026-09). The snapshot supports allowing different lengths for different jobs; it doesn't rank formats or define a success threshold.

The same observed surface mixed AI and career subjects with Git, productivity, community, challenges, and engineering (measured, DEV Top Month, n=surface snapshot, 2026-09). Don't assume an emotional stance or topic dominates organic performance (directional).

Loibner inspected a checked-in Forem experiment, explicitly not a live production trace. In that variant:

- The comments factor was 0.15 at zero comments and 0.66 at two (measured, Loibner, n=single public variant, 2026-07).
- Author-follow factors were 1.0 for followed authors and 0.01 otherwise for signed-in readers; user-specific factors were skipped for anonymous readers (measured, Loibner, n=single public variant, 2026-07).
- Age factors were 1.0 on publication day, 0.2 on day seven, and 0.02 on day eight; candidate eligibility lasted about two weeks (measured, Loibner, n=single public variant, 2026-07).
- Other modeled inputs included reactions, tag/language matching, recommendations, feed-success, clickbait, and randomness (measured, Loibner, n=single public variant, 2026-07).

Use this as a reason to serve returning readers and stay available after publication. Don't convert isolated factors into a reach multiplier, guaranteed expiry, or claim about Top ordering (directional).

### Re-deriving a baseline

Review your own results weekly; refresh external comparison samples monthly and before reusing their claims (directional). Preserve capture time and timezone, article identity, publication age, format, language, tags, counts, and selection method. Include ordinary eligible posts rather than only Top winners (directional).

Compare your own median and spread at matched ages and disclose sample size and missing values. Keep challenge or official-team posts separate when comparing independent publishing. Judge useful comments by their content: reproduction attempts, relevant constraints, and requests from plausible users matter more than generic praise (directional).

If collecting via API, verify current documentation, authentication, pagination, and parameter semantics before running the collection. No endpoint parameters or authentication assumptions are established by the retained evidence. Keep raw responses and the exact collection method; use a documented manual sample if API access isn't verified (directional).

Keep destination metrics separate from native attention. Google documents `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` for consistent campaign attribution, with `utm_content` for link variations (official, Google URL Builder, 2026-09). For DEV, distinguish article-body versus sidebar placement and record downstream actions without crediting every launch-day conversion to this source (directional).

## Additional format examples

All examples below are illustrative. Brackets require the author's supplied evidence; the draft isn't publishable until filled. Promotional material still requires the main skill's policy gate (directional).

### AMA invitation

> Title: Maintaining the accessible timetable for a community venue
>
> I maintain [venue's timetable] and can discuss the keyboard testing documented at [record]. [Observed behavior] is the part we can reproduce so far; we haven't validated [untested setup]. I'll be here during [actual availability and timezone] to answer implementation questions. I can't speak for users we haven't interviewed.

Why it works: the AMA format declares a verifiable remit and real availability.  
The provenance rule keeps the author from claiming to represent unconsulted users.

### Challenge entry

For challenges announced after 2026-08-05, the default is one submission per person per prompt unless the announcement or FAQ grants an exception. Projects must be built inside the challenge window unless exempted; post-deadline commits are excluded and need clear README notes to avoid disqualification (official, DEV Challenge Updates, 2026-08).

Read the current announcement and use its required template, tags, and eligibility rules. Confirm repository timestamps and identify the judged revision; document later work separately. Don't recycle a launch article that fails the submission requirements (directional).

> Title: A keyboard-first shift planner for the community kitchen
>
> Prompt: [official prompt]. Submission: [demo and judged revision].
>
> The supplied schedule fixture shows [observed behavior] when someone changes a shift without a pointer. [Required technology] handles [specific responsibility]. The build record covers [eligible window]; [README note] identifies work after the deadline. Mobile screen-reader behavior remains untested.

Why it works: the entry exposes eligibility and reproducible work directly.  
The limitation rule avoids presenting a desktop fixture as complete accessibility evidence.

### Replacing a vague body with sourced scope

Weak draft, illustrative:

> This covers everything needed to make a booking form accessible.

Revised fragment, illustrative:

> This note covers the focus return described in [test record]. Start with [fixture link] and compare the highlighted control before and after the slot disappears. Validation messages remain outside this test, so the result doesn't establish that the whole form is accessible.

Why it works: the revision replaces an unsupported completeness claim with an inspectable task.  
The proof rule names what the check cannot establish without fabricating a result.

## Voice evidence boundaries

Use the practitioner watchlists only to locate a reader-facing problem such as repetition or an obscured claim. They don't establish DEV ranking penalties or prove machine authorship. Keep a useful question, three-part explanation, recap, or dash; don't replace a clear passage to reduce its pattern count. Aborn also rejects deliberate typos as a solution; preserve register instead (directional).

For storytelling, retaining unresolved costs is an editorial inference from fiction research, not a validated social-post detector (directional). StoryScope reports AI fiction favoring explicit themes and tidy plots (measured, StoryScope, n=61,608 stories, 2026-08).

Use actual decisions and their documented costs when adapting Lees's practitioner advice about specificity beyond its original LinkedIn context (directional). For translated posts, have a fluent editor review idioms in context; this is an inference from the MIDI language-comprehension study, not a DEV engagement result (directional).

Judge drafts against records and author voice instead of optimizing an AI-detector score. Professional editing moved scores in conflicting directions across detectors in an academic-text study (measured, manuscript-editing preprint, n=135,389 manuscript pairs, 2026-08). It supplies no short-post error rate.

No DEV-specific format comparison comes from the cross-network vendor reports here. Don't import another network's reply lift, carousel advantage, media labels, or voting policy as a DEV fact.
