## Overview

Fundraising outreach is a sales motion with a small, researched list and a long relationship tail. This skill gives the reusable structures: how to score investor fit, how to ask for a warm intro, how to write the cold email, when to follow up, how to write the update email, and what to track. It produces drafts only; the founder sends everything.

## Mental model

An investor reads for three things in about thirty seconds: is this in my lane, is there evidence it is working, and is the ask clear and small. Every message answers those three and nothing else. A warm intro beats a cold email because someone else has already vouched; a cold email beats a vague warm one when it is specific and short.

## Rules

### Investor fit scoring

Score each candidate from sourced facts only. Never state a portfolio company, check size, or thesis you did not find in a cited source.

| Criterion | Evidence to find | Weight |
|-----------|------------------|--------|
| Stage | Recent rounds they led or joined at your stage | High |
| Check size | Stated range or comparable recent deals | High |
| Thesis and sector | Their writing, talks, and portfolio pattern | High |
| Geography | Stated geography or recent deals nearby | Medium |
| Conflicts | Portfolio companies competing with you directly | Blocker if direct |
| Activity | A deal or post in the last 12 months | Medium |
| Path in | Shared connection, portfolio founder, event | Decides warm vs cold |

Output a tiered list: tier 1 (best fit, warm path), tier 2 (good fit, cold), tier 3 (stretch). Keep each source as a URL next to the claim. Mark anything unverified as unverified.

### Warm-intro request

Two parts: a short note to the connector, and a forwardable blurb they can paste.

Note to connector: one line of context, one line on why this investor, the explicit ask ("would you be comfortable introducing me?"), and an easy out ("no problem if not").

Forwardable blurb, 80 words or fewer, written in third person so the connector can forward it without editing:

- Who the founder is and one credibility fact.
- What the company does in one plain sentence.
- One traction proof point with a number.
- Why this investor, specifically.
- The ask: a 20-minute intro call.

### Cold investor email

Target under 150 words. Structure:

1. Subject: specific and plain, for example "Seed: [one-line what] — [one traction number]". No hype, no all-caps.
2. Line one: why this investor, citing a real, sourced signal (a post, a portfolio company, a stated thesis).
3. What you do, one sentence.
4. Proof: one or two numbers (revenue, growth, users, pilots). Honest numbers; no invented metrics.
5. The round: stage and size if known, nothing more.
6. One ask: a short call, or permission to send the deck.

Don't attach the deck on first contact unless asked. Link to it instead.

### Follow-up cadence

| Touch | Timing | Content |
|-------|--------|---------|
| 1 | Day 0 | Initial email or intro |
| 2 | Day 5-7 | Short bump with one new proof point |
| 3 | Day 14 | New milestone or a useful artifact |
| Stop | After touch 3 | Close the loop politely; add to the update list |

A reply of "not now" moves the investor to the update list. A clear "no" ends outreach. Never send more than three unanswered touches.

### Investor update email

Monthly or quarterly, to existing investors and to interested ones who said "keep me posted". Keep it scannable:

- Subject: "[Company] update — [Month]: [headline number]".
- Headline: the single most important result.
- Metrics: three to five, same ones every time, with change since last update.
- Wins and misses: both, plainly. Bad news early builds trust.
- Asks: specific ("two intros to Series A investors in fintech"), numbered.
- Next period: top three priorities.

### Tracking sheet columns

Investor, firm, tier, fit score, source URLs, path in (warm or cold), connector, date of touch 1, touch 2, touch 3, status (no reply, replied, meeting, passed, interested), next step, next step date, notes.

### Securities communication caution

Fundraising messages are securities communications. Don't promise or imply returns, don't use guarantee language, don't claim a round is oversubscribed unless it is, and keep forward-looking statements clearly labelled as plans. Terms, instruments, and the way offers are made vary by jurisdiction; send them to the founder's counsel for review. This is drafting help, not legal or investment advice.

## Examples

Bad subject: "Revolutionary AI startup seeking investment!!!"

Good subject: "Seed: AI invoice matching, $18k MRR, 14% MoM"

Bad opener: "I think you will love what we are building."

Good opener: "Your March post on boring-vertical SaaS matched what we saw when we hit 40 accounts in accounting firms."

Bad claim: "Backed by top investors" with no names.

Good claim: Name the investor, with their permission, or drop the claim.

## Checklist

- [ ] Every investor fact traces to a source URL, or is marked unverified?
- [ ] No direct portfolio conflict left in tiers 1 and 2?
- [ ] Cold email under 150 words with one specific ask?
- [ ] Forwardable blurb readable without edits by the connector?
- [ ] No more than three unanswered touches planned per investor?
- [ ] Update email has the same metrics as last time, plus misses?
- [ ] No return promises, no fake scarcity, counsel pointer present for terms?

## Composition / References

- Pair with a pitch-deck specialist when a deck is the missing artifact.
- Pair with a validation specialist when traction claims need market evidence first.
- Check current round-size and valuation data from live sources; do not quote benchmarks from memory.
