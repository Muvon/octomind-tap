## Use these examples

Open this reference when drafting a thread, longer explanation, profile bio, or casual-chain reply. All briefs and outcomes below are fictional illustrations. Don't reuse a personal claim without equivalent supplied evidence. Bracketed specifics are permitted in teaching drafts only and make those drafts unpublishable until filled and verified.

The weak versions illustrate a substantive editing problem without supplying a reusable bait hook. The revised versions are shapes to study, not hooks to copy.

## Thread: independent evidence

Illustrative brief: a developer-tool maintainer writes calmly, has an actual patch preview and test output, and hasn't checked generated files.

Weak version:

> The rename preview is available.

Follow-up:

> The preview is ready to try.

This repeats the announcement instead of adding information.

Revised root:

> The rename preview shows the proposed diff before it writes to disk. The recording uses a small example project. Generated files are excluded for now. Demo: [URL].

Revised continuation:

> The test output covers imports and ordinary references. It doesn't cover names constructed at runtime, so review those separately before accepting a rename.

Why it works: the thread rule gives the continuation its own useful evidence.
The provenance rule bounds the demonstration and avoids a universal safety claim.

## Longer post: retain the conditions

Illustrative brief: an operations consultant has permission to discuss a pilot. Their voice is formal but plain; measurement values still need source records.

Weak version:

> Our approval workflow makes invoice processing easier. Contact us to discuss the workflow.

This withholds the information a buyer needs to evaluate the claim.

Revised longer-post teaching draft:

> The invoice pilot covered [customer-approved cohort] during [period]. Review time changed from [baseline] to [observed result], using the same definition of review time throughout.
>
> Routing changed during the pilot. Invoices with matched purchase orders went to the designated approver; disputed invoices stayed in manual review. Staffing also changed during the period, so we can't attribute the whole difference to the routing change.
>
> This may suit teams whose invoices already contain reliable purchase-order references. It doesn't resolve disputes or repair incomplete source records. The approved method is at [source URL].
>
> If that describes your workflow, the evaluation form is at [destination URL].

Why it works: continuous reading keeps the measurement conditions beside the result.
The launch-proof rule gives a buyer an explicit fit boundary and a usable action.

## Bio: match the work

Illustrative brief: a repair-shop owner writes directly and serves a local audience. Their service scope and booking page are supplied.

Weak version:

> Bicycle repair business. Read our posts about bicycles.

Revised:

> I repair commuter bikes in [neighborhood]. Notes from the workshop, including repairs that aren't worth paying for. Bookings: [approved destination].

Why it works: the profile rule states real work and a concrete reason to follow.
The provenance rule requires the owner to approve the service promise and destination.

## Casual-chain reply: register without errors

Illustrative brief: a houseplant creator naturally writes in lowercase. A reader asks whether a pictured yellow leaf proves overwatering; the creator has no root or soil evidence.

Weak version:

> The yellow leaf means you're overwatering the plant.

Revised:

> i'd check whether the pot is draining before changing the watering routine. the yellow leaf alone doesn't tell me enough

Why it works: documented lowercase register carries over without invented typos or slang.
The evidence rule preserves uncertainty and offers an observable next step.

## Founder decision: disclose the cost

Illustrative brief: a scheduling-tool founder has documented a delayed calendar integration; the existing manual export works. Their approved voice is concise and matter-of-fact.

> I'm holding the calendar integration until we can explain failed syncs in the app. The manual export is still available. That means teams needing automatic updates should wait; the current version won't cover that workflow.

Why it works: the decision shape names a real constraint and its buyer consequence.
The author-voice rule allows a position without a moral closer or manufactured drama.

## Review voice and substance

Use `content-voice` for generic editing. On X, inspect whether compression has replaced meaning with filler, empty suspense, or a closing slogan. Review emphasis words and punctuation in context; retain real contrasts, useful lists, ordinary questions, and summaries that help the reader. These practitioner heuristics aren't word bans, measured X reach penalties, or proof of authorship (directional; Aborn, Cox, Gichigi).

Aborn rejects deliberate typos as evidence of human authorship. Keep documented register, including distinctive punctuation, without injecting mistakes (directional), citing Aborn, 2026-07. Lees recommends a real defended decision and its cost; use actual source records for that specificity (directional), citing Lees, 2026-08.

StoryScope finds tidier, more explicitly explained narrative patterns in generated fiction. Reviewing a social post for a miracle-fix parable is an extrapolation, not a validated social-post detector (measured, StoryScope preprint, n=61,608 stories, 2026-08). Preserve unresolved tradeoffs because they belong to the evidence.

MIDI finds conversational context helps idiom comprehension without eliminating language disparities. Ask a fluent local reviewer to check uncertain slang in context; that editing recommendation is an inference (measured, MIDI preprint, n=18 languages, 2026-06).

Human manuscript editing changed detector scores in conflicting directions across systems; the research didn't study short social posts (measured, manuscript-detector preprint, n=135,389 manuscript pairs across 13 detectors, 2026-08). Don't use a detector threshold as the publishing gate. Check drafts, source records, and author approval (directional).
