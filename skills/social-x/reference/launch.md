## Scope

Open this reference for an X launch plan, benchmark comparison, collaboration brief, or outcome report. Keep cross-platform campaign sequencing outside this skill. Use the main playbook's intake, post shapes, and illustrative launch-week schedule.

## Select evidence and a conversion path

Match proof to the buying situation. A technical evaluator needs a reproducible artifact with conditions; a buyer deciding fit needs an honest boundary and usable next step (directional). Obtain the source record before writing any personal experience, price, timing, or customer result. If it isn't available, report the missing evidence instead of filling a story with plausible details.

For demos, show the task's actual starting state and the relevant action. Disclose edits that change the viewer's interpretation, including omitted waiting or human intervention. Preserve enough context to interpret a screenshot. For proof, record baseline, measurement window, exclusions, and customer permission. Avoid implying that the tool alone caused a change when the source cannot isolate it (directional).

Keep the announcement usable when forwarded outside the feed. Include what the product does, whom it fits, and the qualification needed to evaluate the claim. This is craft advice, not a deduction that copy-link weights make a specific format optimal (directional).

## Prepare participation before announcement

Monitor exact problem phrases and answer where the product's expertise fits. Save recurring objections as source material; don't turn every reply into a link drop (directional). Sprout's undated listening guide recommends tracking frustrations, buyer-journey leaks, expansion opportunities, and competitor positioning; these are practitioner uses of listening, not measured X conversion effects (directional).

The MailTest founder's launch account reports negligible X exposure without an existing audience and a subsequent plan to use pain-phrase searches and helpful participation. Use it as a caution about distribution preparation, not proof that all cold launches fail (directional), citing founder self-report, April 20, 2026. Begin with communities and people already relevant to the work.

Select collaborators by audience fit and relevant expertise. Clickstrike's Patronus AI case reports matched creator selection; its Neverbell case separately tracks creator-post results and wider campaign reach. Both are agency self-reports without controlled conversion evidence (directional), citing Clickstrike, undated. Brief the actual product and proof, let participants write from their experience, and distinguish each account's results. Don't copy agency view totals into a forecast.

State founder and employee affiliation. Use the Paid Partnership toggle for compensated, gifted, affiliate, and ambassador promotions (official, X Paid Partnerships, 2026-09). Don't buy or exchange metrics, require copy-pasted reactions, or send unsolicited promotional replies and DMs in bulk (official, X Authenticity, 2025-04).

If the X post sends readers to Product Hunt, ask for product evaluation without vote requests or rewards (official, Product Hunt sharing guide, 2026-09). If it points to Show HN, don't recruit friends to comment or vote (official, Show HN, 2026-09). Follow the destination's rules without importing its format into X.

## Staff the response

Assign a person who can answer product questions. Record unresolved issues and return with evidence. Use a public reply when the answer helps other readers; move sensitive customer details into a permitted private conversation. Don't fake personal responses with repeated generated variations (directional).

Use a fresh original when an objection deserves its own self-contained explanation. A reply addresses the people already in that conversation; don't promise broader For You discovery for it (official, X README OON filtering, 2026-08). Continue follow-up after announcement when actual use produces something new. Repeating the same pitch doesn't add proof.

## Measure qualified interest

Define success from the brief before comparing post variants. Record available native observations such as relevant replies, follows, profile activity, destination clicks, and permission-based DM inquiries. Mark unobservable metrics as unavailable, not zero. Review what people asked and whether they fit the buying situation (directional).

Separate downstream visits and signups from native observations. Continue through activation or qualified meetings when those records exist; don't treat likes or congratulatory comments as demand. DooCloud's account of broad channel activity with weak paid uptake is an unaudited caution, not a channel ranking or proof that trust caused the result (directional), citing founder report, May 27, 2026.

Use consistent `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign`; use `utm_content` to distinguish creatives or link placements (official, Google Analytics URL builder, 2026-09). Compare the same observation windows and keep known tier differences visible. Record campaign totals separately from results attributable to an individual X post.

Chartbeat documents referral loss from apps and messages in its news-media network; it doesn't establish SaaS conversion attribution (measured, Chartbeat, n=not established here; scope=Chartbeat news-media network traffic, January data reviewed 2026-09). Keep the explanation understandable when forwarded (directional). Retain raw self-reported discovery alongside tracked visits and allow unknown origin. HockeyStack's documented example groups reported company and personal LinkedIn sources into an organic bucket; using that pattern for X is an adaptation, not validation of recall accuracy (directional).
