## Overview

Open this reference when preparing private source notes, checking linked-title derivation, or helping a human author evaluate an objection. These are illustrative exercises with no claimed historical performance. They extend the main skill's worked examples without supplying prose for publication.

The human writes the HN text independently from evidence. Don't present an AI rewrite as a compliant intermediate draft. Show HN moderator guidance explicitly rejects LLM-generated or edited text, including small edits (official, Show HN Tips, 2026-09).

## Source-note handoff: developer tool

Illustrative supplied brief: a maintainer has built a dependency-diff viewer. The sample report demonstrates supported manifests; the supplied limitation is that private registries haven't been evaluated. The desired action is to inspect the sample before installing locally. No install-speed or security claims are supplied.

Private writing beats:
- Identify the report artifact and its source URL in the brief.
- Describe the visible comparison the reader can inspect.
- Explain the maintainer's reason only from supplied notes; request missing history instead of writing a personal anecdote.
- Preserve the private-registry limitation and the actual installation path.

Why it works: the artifact supplies specificity under the format rule.
The provenance rule prevents invented production use or security outcomes.

## Linked-title derivation: B2B engineering article

Illustrative source headline: “Why we moved our task queue to Postgres - Queue Notes”. “Queue Notes” is the site name in this exercise.

Rejected candidate: “The definitive guide to replacing message brokers”.
Accepted derivation: “Why we moved our task queue to Postgres”.

Why it works: original-title handling permits removal of the site name (official, HN Guidelines, 2026-09).
The accepted version retains the source's bounded decision instead of claiming a universal recommendation.

Don't remove “Postgres” because a domain happens to contain that word. The subject of the article remains meaningful; the removable suffix is the site name.

## Objection handoff: commercial product

Illustrative situation: a reader asks whether the knitting-chart export is usable without a paid account. The supplied pricing page distinguishes sample export from paid saved projects, and the author has confirmed that an unsupported notation remains unsupported.

Rejected approach: imply export is unrestricted, move the paid requirement into a later reply, or call the concern a misunderstanding.

Private reply beats:
- Answer from the current pricing page and identify the free evaluation path.
- State what requires payment before describing saved projects.
- Preserve the notation limitation; don't promise a release date without an approved commitment.

Why it works: the launch-readiness rule makes commercial terms visible at evaluation.
The reply addresses the actual objection without invented reassurance.

## Ask HN handoff: local repair service

Illustrative question candidate: “Ask HN: How do repair shops schedule work with uncertain parts arrivals?”

Private writing beats:
- Supply [shop relationship] and [current scheduling approach].
- Explain [attempt already made] and [observed failure] from shop records.
- Ask for experience handling delayed parts while preserving customer commitments.
- Omit the shop's booking link and any disguised request to assess its product.

Why it works: the Ask HN format asks for experience under a real constraint.
Bracketed facts block publication until supplied; they aren't permission to improvise a shop history.

## Proof handoff: customer outcome

Illustrative evidence worksheet:

| Field | Evidence required |
|---|---|
| Starting condition | [baseline record and measurement definition] |
| Changed result | [result record under comparable conditions] |
| Observation window | [date range] |
| Alternative explanations | [other operational changes during the comparison] |
| Unresolved limitation | [remaining failure or cost] |
| Permission and relationship | [customer permission and author affiliation] |

Rejected approach: frame the outcome as an instant transformation, omit another simultaneous process change, or convert a customer's paraphrased remark into a quote.

Why it works: the proof-post rule requires a checkable comparison with limits.
The strip test leaves operational knowledge useful even without the product name.

## Checklist

- [ ] Every exercise remains marked illustrative; none is cited as a real winner.
- [ ] The human has source notes, not a polished generated comment to paste.
- [ ] Missing personal history, customer permission, and measurement records block publication.
- [ ] Title derivations preserve the original claim and identify only permitted changes.
- [ ] Limitations remain visible; no miracle-fix ending, rhetorical cadence, or manufactured errors appear.
