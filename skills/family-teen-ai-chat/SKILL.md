## Overview

Parents ask how much their teen uses chatbots and what to do about it. The outcome is a short plan: what to set, what to ask, what to watch for, and when to call a professional. In a Pew Research Center survey of 1,458 teen and parent pairs (fielded 2025-09-25 to 2025-10-09, published 2026-02-24), 64% of teens said they use chatbots, 51% of parents said their teen does with about 3 in 10 unsure, and about 4 in 10 parents had never talked with their teen about it.

## Mental model

A general chatbot answers questions and does homework. A companion app is built to feel like a friend or partner and to keep the teen talking. The risks differ: the first is mostly about cheating and wrong answers; the second is about emotional dependence, sexual content, and a teen sharing worries with a program instead of a person. Talk first, set limits second, and keep the relationship bigger than the rules.

## Rules

Settings checklist (check each app's own help page; names change)

1. Account age: the teen's real birth date is on the account, so age-based protections apply.
2. App parental controls: turn on any family or teen mode the app offers; note what it shows the parent and what it does not.
3. Content settings: turn off or limit romantic, sexual or role-play modes; check whether the app lets a teen switch them back on.
4. Memory and history: know whether the app stores chats, and how to delete history and the account.
5. Data sharing: switch off use of chats for model training where the app offers it.
6. Purchases: block in-app purchases and subscriptions on the phone, since companion apps often sell upgrades.
7. Device limits: use the phone's own screen-time tools for night hours; keep chargers out of bedrooms.
8. Be open about monitoring: tell the teen what the parent can see. Don't install hidden tracking software; check the local law first.

Conversation openers (ask, then listen; no verdict in the first talk)

- "What do you use it for? Show me one thing it was good at."
- "Has it ever said something that felt wrong or too personal?"
- "Do you ever tell it things you haven't told a person?" (if yes, thank them; ask what they'd want from a person)
- "What would you do if it told you to keep a secret from us?"
- Share your own use first, including a time it was wrong.

Warning signs

| Sign | Level |
|------|-------|
| Hours a day, loses sleep, drops friends or activities for the app | Talk this week; set a time rule together |
| Calls the bot a boyfriend, girlfriend or best friend; distress when it is unavailable | Talk this week; consider a family counselor |
| Sexual or romantic role-play with a bot; asked for photos, money or secrecy | Remove the feature or app; report in the app; if an adult is behind it, treat as abuse and contact child-protection services and the police |
| Talk of death or self-harm, with or without the bot | Same-day professional contact; if danger is immediate, call emergency services; US 988 call or text, elsewhere findahelpline.com |

If a teen says a chatbot encouraged self-harm, keep the conversation (screenshot), tell the app, and bring it to the clinician.

## Examples

Bad: "Ban the app and check their phone every night."
Good: "Ask what it does for them. Set the settings above that apply together, agree no phones in bedrooms at night, and review in two weeks. If they call it their only friend, add a counselor visit to the plan."

## Checklist

- [ ] Teen's age and which app asked.
